Why I built IMD TV

Complete, free, and fast.

IMD TV exists because there are limited free IPTV applications that offer a complete feature set without requiring a licence, subscription or locking essential functionality behind a paywall. Most of the open-source alternatives available today sit outside the Android ecosystem, and more specifically, Android TV. IMD TV was built to address that gap, bringing a fully featured, open-source IPTV application to the platform.

Speed is the primary focus of IMD TV, and it's something that has influenced every part of its design. Watching TV, browsing the EPG, switching channels and searching through playlists should all be quick and responsive, regardless of the device you're using. These are fundamental parts of the viewing experience, and they shouldn't be accompanied by the delays that have become commonplace in other IPTV applications.

The rest follows from that. Every screen is designed for the remote first. It works with any provider's Xtream login, M3U link or Stalker portal rather than one service. Your logins stay encrypted on your box, and nothing about what you watch leaves it. The best part? It's tested and developed on budget streaming sticks first, not just high-end boxes.

Key features

Everything, one remote away.

Keep watching while you browse

Leave full screen and the channel carries on in the guide's preview while you look for what's next.

  • Preview channels as you scroll, or with one press of OK
  • Opens on the last channel you watched
  • Dolby and DTS sound on any box with the built-in decoder, or passed straight to your TV

A guide that fills itself in

Seven days of programmes, with free open-source guides for the channels your provider leaves blank. You can even choose EPG priority.

  • Borrows a guide from similar channels: “Sport HEVC” uses “Sport”
  • “Use guide from…” gives any channel another channel's guide
  • Add your own guide sources and shift each one's times
  • Updates by itself: on a timer, when the app opens, or when your playlist changes

Your channels, your way

Big playlists become manageable: keep what you watch, hide the rest.

  • Favourites, and categories of your own: copy channels in, place them above or below the provider's
  • Rename channels and your own categories
  • Hide and reorder categories, or hide a whole language or country at once
  • Manage channels: show, hide and reorder them, or tick many and hide or show them all at once
  • Clean up names: remove prefixes and suffixes such as “UK|”, strip symbols such as “ᴿᴬᵂ” and “◉” – you can even hide divider channels
  • Merge duplicates: the HD, 4K and RAW versions of a channel show as one, playing the best one – or merge any two channels yourself

Movies and series

Posters, seasons and episodes, and resume where you left off – from your playlist and your Plex, Jellyfin and Stremio libraries.

  • Subtitles in your language, when the film or episode has them

Search everything

One search for channels, films, series and TV guide programmes, with results as you type. Programmes on now play in one press; later ones can be recorded.

Record live TV

Hold OK on a channel to record what's on now, or pick a later programme from its guide.

  • Save to the box, a USB drive or a network share
  • Starts a little early and ends a little late, because guide times drift
  • Keeps the box awake while it records
  • Watch your recordings from the Recordings tab

Get IMD TV

On your TV in minutes.

Option 1: Downloader (on the TV)

  1. On your TV, install the free Downloader app from the Play Store.
  2. Open Downloader, type the code 7573990 and press Go. When asked, allow Downloader to install apps, then choose Install.
  3. Open IMD TV and add your Xtream login, M3U link or Stalker portal.

Option 2: Sideloading (from a computer)

  1. Download imd-tv.apk on your computer.
  2. On the TV, turn on Developer options and USB or network debugging, then run adb connect <your TV's IP> and adb install imd-tv.apk. Or copy the file to a USB drive and open it with a file manager app on the TV.
  3. Open IMD TV and add your Xtream login, M3U link or Stalker portal.

Questions

Good to know.

Does IMD TV come with channels?

No. IMD TV is a player: it plays the playlist you already have from your provider. It doesn't sell, host or link to any channels or content.

Which boxes does it work on?

Android TV and Google TV 7.1 or newer (Fire TV: Fire OS 6 or newer). It's tested on a Chromecast with Google TV and a Thomson 140 streaming stick, and it's designed to stay quick on budget hardware.

Which playlists does it support?

Xtream logins (server, username and password), M3U links, and Stalker portals (portal URL and MAC address; beta), from any provider. M3U channels protected with ClearKey or Widevine DRM play too. You can add more than one playlist.

Can I record one channel and watch another?

Only if your provider allows two connections at once. Xtream logins tell the app their limit, and it asks before stopping anything. M3U playlists and Stalker portals don't, so the recording fails if the provider refuses a second stream. Recording the channel you're watching needs two connections too.

How much can I record?

Small boxes have little room: a Chromecast with Google TV holds about 2 hours of HD. Use a USB drive or a network share for more. Recordings on the box or a USB drive are removed if you uninstall the app; recordings on a network share stay.

Will a scheduled recording wake my box?

Not always. A box in deep standby may miss the start, and the recording is marked "Missed". If the box wakes while the programme is still on, the rest is recorded.

What can't it record?

DASH and encrypted streams. Recordings are saved as they arrive (.ts files), not converted. There's no live pause and no series recording.

Is it really free?

Yes, with no ads, subscriptions or paid features. If you'd like to say thanks, there's an optional coffee link below.

What data does it collect?

None. There are no accounts or analytics. The app only talks to your provider, to the guide and logo sources you choose, and to GitHub for open-source logos.
